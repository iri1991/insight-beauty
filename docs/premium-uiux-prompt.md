# Insight Beauty — premium UI/UX reference pull

**Structural DNA:** chapter gates: each major product area enters through a calm, editorial threshold rather than a dashboard wall of equal-weight cards.

**Experience principle:** clinical confidence without clinical coldness. The product should feel like a well-run skin studio: measured, private, tactile, and legible in a few seconds.

**Reference cues kept:** Aesop's material restraint and warm editorial rhythm; Augustinus Bader's skin-science framing; boutique clinic layouts that use whitespace as a signal of calm expertise.

**Reference cues deliberately excluded:** cosmetic "before / after" theatrics, glassmorphism, floating gradient blobs, generic wellness photography, and faux clinical metrics.

**Palette:** mineral paper `#F5F1EA`, chalk `#FCFAF6`, lichen ink `#18211F`, muted sage `#93A596`, and terracotta signal `#C96C55` for consequential actions and states.

**Typography:** Instrument Serif carries the human, consultative moments; DM Sans handles task UI; DM Mono labels clinical states, timestamps, and system metadata.

**Composition:** generous breathing room, strong left-edge alignment, 1px mineral rules, and one dominant surface per screen. Cards exist only when they contain a distinct task, not as decoration.

**Signature visual:** a layered “dermal lens” object: an abstract skin-profile analysis built with dimensional CSS planes and contour lines. It communicates insight and progression without stock imagery or literal anatomy.

**Motion:** quiet and directional. Sections are uncovered with an editorial mask; controls acknowledge input through border and elevation changes only. No bounce, no upward fade-in, no perpetual motion outside the dermal lens's slow optical drift.

**Accessibility and responsive intent:** high contrast on editorial dark surfaces, descriptive labels beside visual-only data, keyboard-visible focus states, and layouts that collapse into a deliberate single-column reading order.

**Product trust:** no fake demonstrations, invented counts, or unimplemented clinical promises. Landing actions lead to the actual authenticated product flow; clinical outputs remain framed as support for professional consultation.
